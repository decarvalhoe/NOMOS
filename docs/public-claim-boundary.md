# Public Claim Boundary

This document defines what Nomos can honestly claim at `v0.1.0-ALPHA`.

Product delivery, DevOps tooling and regulated assurance have independent
statuses (ADR-VRC-0004, [roadmap router](47-roadmap-lanes-and-risk-based-validation.md)).
A green product capability or a tested supporting tool does not establish QMS
effectiveness or validation for a regulated intended use. A missing human
record, calendar run, licence decision or external activation locks its named
regulated claim; it does not make unrelated product work incomplete.

## Allowed Claims

Nomos may claim that it:

- implements a canonical-first method for source-to-product traceability;
- provides a Go CLI for project diagnosis and corpus processing;
- can scan, manifest, diff, validate sidecars, feed, produce a body ledger, run strict gates, and attest source corpora;
- has a working `rbok-lawbook` profile for structured Markdown reference corpora;
- can produce certified TOC artifacts, governed lexicon output, source-backed RAG metadata, runtime import artifacts, body-ledger evidence, release gate results, fidelity proof reports, and attestations;
- has been exercised on a real RBOK lawbook POC with 7191 feed nodes and a green strict fidelity gate;
- has been exercised on a bounded RBOK `01_rbok` source-to-feed POC with 3024 feed units, 3024 RAG chunks, 3024/3024 source-backed units/chunks, zero uncovered body-ledger bytes, and zero semantic blocking findings;
- includes regulated-readiness documentation, templates, evidence-pack automation, and a GitHub operating model.

## Conditional Claims

Nomos may claim the following only with context:

- **"Regulated-ready"** means the project has a regulated-readiness structure and evidence workflow; it does not mean certified or validated for a customer's regulated use.
- **"Full fidelity proven"** applies to a specific POC output and gate configuration, not every possible corpus and document format.
- **"Source-integrity-proven"** applies only to a recorded run whose source integrity, feed quality, semantic blocking count, body ledger, and strict gate all pass.
- **"Read-only corpus processing"** applies when the guard runs against a clean source repository and the source mutation check passes.
- **"RAG-ready"** means traceable metadata exists; it does not mean production vector-store retrieval and LLM behavior have been validated.

## Prohibited Claims At This Release

Do not claim that Nomos is:

- FDA validated;
- 21 CFR Part 11 compliant as a platform;
- EU Annex 11 compliant as a platform;
- GxP validated;
- ISO certified;
- NASA qualified;
- a complete eQMS;
- a substitute for customer validation;
- universally faithful across all PDFs, DOCX, images, scanned documents, legal codes, regulations, or game-rule books without corpus-specific evidence;
- legally authorized to redistribute licensed standards;
- cleared for trademark use or registration without a recorded trademark clearance decision;
- backed by a freedom-to-operate opinion for RAG governance, patent-search automation, or source-grounded retrieval;
- cleared for unrestricted third-party license use without a recorded third-party license clearance review.

## Evidence Rule

Every public claim must map to one of:

- a passing automated gate;
- a generated artifact;
- a reviewed document;
- a controlled decision;
- a known gap with owner and next action.

If a claim cannot be mapped, it must be removed or rewritten as a roadmap item.

## What NOMOS Proves Today

At `v0.1.0-ALPHA`, NOMOS proves only:

- that it can read a source corpus, build a manifest, and detect drift between source and recorded hashes;
- that it can generate corpus feed artifacts, certified TOC, governed lexicon output, source-backed RAG metadata, runtime import artifacts, body-ledger evidence, fidelity proof reports, and attestations from the configured profile;
- that, on the specific RBOK lawbook POC corpus and configuration, the existing strict fidelity gate has reported `full_fidelity_proven` for the recorded run;
- that, on the recorded RBOK `01_rbok` source-to-feed POC (`C:\Dev\nomos-rbok-poc-run-20260504-structured-universal-9`), source integrity, feed quality, body ledger, and strict gate passed with zero semantic blocking findings.

These are all **artifact-generation** facts and **POC-scoped** facts. They are not, on their own, a platform-wide source-to-feed fidelity proof.

## What NOMOS Does Not Yet Prove

NOMOS does **not** yet prove, at platform scope:

- complete source-to-feed coverage across arbitrary corpora;
- absence of all semantic warnings across arbitrary corpora;
- support for every PDF, DOCX, scanned document, image, legal code, regulation, rule book, or customer-specific format;
- customer-specific regulated validation, intended-use approval, supplier qualification, or certification;
- trademark clearance for the NOMOS name or related wordmarks;
- a freedom-to-operate opinion for implementation or public claims;
- third-party license clearance for dependencies that require OSS or counsel review.

The remaining proof chain is release-scoped: repeated CI evidence on private corpora, broader document-format adapters, and customer validation packs.

Attestation `claim_coverage` is wired (VRC-07 #553): `nomos corpus attest --corpus-body-ledger` verifies the ledger's Merkle inclusion proofs (recomputing every leaf from the ledger rows) and computes `claim_coverage` from the verified ledger; `nomos corpus body-ledger --verify` exposes the same verification standalone. A tampered ledger fails the attestation (adversarial tests in tree). This is a tree capability claim; recorded POC dossiers keep their original run-scoped statements.

RAG interop export is wired (#614): `nomos rag export` emits chunk records any RAG stack can index and later cite (`embedding_text` carries a deterministic structural context prefix; `body_text` is the citable source text and never contains that prefix), refusing any chunk without `chunk_id`, `source_id`, `source_hash`, or body; `nomos rag manifest` fingerprints the export per source and per chunk so index staleness is provable against the source hash; `nomos rag delta` turns two manifests into the exact per-chunk plan (embed / update_metadata / delete) and `nomos rag verify` gates an index manifest against the current feed (exit 1 when stale; a hand-edited manifest whose digest does not match its own chunk list is refused as a baseline). Exports are byte-deterministic (no wall clock is read). With `--bundle`, records carry the node facets; `--lens` enforces a Knowledge Lens on the corpus handed to the index (excluded chunks are named and counted; a facet-less chunk under a lens is excluded), the manifest binds the index to the lens digest, a lens change is a full reindex, and the manifest carries a computed retrieval contract that declares temporal scoping unsupported (no record carries effective dates). Adversarial tests are in tree, and `scripts/rag-export-gate.sh` replays determinism, prefix-leak absence, 1-byte staleness detection, the fresh/stale/tampered verify verdicts on the public reference corpus, and the lens verdicts on the real AEC golden bundle against an independent re-implementation of the consumer-kit semantics, in CI. This is a tree capability claim about the export and scope contracts only; Nomos does not rank, and it claims nothing about retrieval quality, embedding behaviour, or LLM answers, which remain governed by the cite-or-abstain gate and the eval harness.

The cite-or-abstain gate's faithfulness proxy is lexical and negation-blind (stated in every verdict). `nomos answer gate|eval --scorer-cmd` (#622) accepts an external NLI scorer as a second judge through a versioned JSON protocol; the verdict is strictest-wins per sentence (a scorer can only tighten the gate, never loosen it) and any scorer failure, timeout or off-contract response fails the answer closed with `FAITHFULNESS_SCORER_FAILED`. `scripts/nomos_hhem_scorer.py` is the reference adapter for HHEM-2.1-Open. No model ships in the Go engine (a wiring-matrix probe keeps it that way) and no CI run scores with a neural model: the claim covers the protocol, the composition direction and the fail-closed behaviour, not NLI accuracy on any corpus.

The RAG answer evidence record consumes that verdict instead of computing its own (#624): `scripts/regulated_rag_answer_evidence.py` runs `nomos answer gate` on the fixtures and takes every score, decision, finding and threshold from the engine, keeping only the evidence envelope (record fields, response contract, confidence range, unique answer ids). In `required` mode (the default, what CI runs) an unavailable or non-verdict engine exits 2 and writes no report at all — a stale one is removed — so no evidence record claims a gate result the engine did not produce. The `fallback` mode is explicit, marked `python_fallback`, warned, capped at the `indicative` tier, and used by no CI gate (a wiring-matrix probe keeps it out of the shell gates). The claim covers verdict provenance and fail-closed behaviour; it is not a claim that any particular answer was correctly judged.

The cite-or-abstain gate is measured by a public bench (#582, VRC-46): `nomos answer bench` runs the gate over a labelled corpus built on the in-repo public reference-basis documents (nine items, one per failure mode: grounded, forged citation, no span text, negation, over-verbosity, prompt injection, unsupported question) and reports the two error directions separately — `false_cite_rate` (an ungrounded answer published as source-backed, the dangerous error) and `must_cite_recall` (over-abstention). The published result of 2026-09-05 (a byte-identical re-publication after the quoted source document was extended; first published 2026-09-04), lexical proxy only: `must_cite_recall` 1.0, `must_abstain_recall` 0.8333, `false_cite_rate` 0.1667 — the single miss is the negation item, the documented blind spot of the lexical proxy, published as a miss. `scripts/cite_or_abstain_bench.py` replays the measurement in CI and fails on any drift: a moved source document, a quote that is not verbatim in its source, a cited reference without a dated verification record, a non-deterministic run, a broken bound, or a published number that no longer matches. This is a measurement of the gate over nine public items; it claims nothing about retrieval quality, LLM accuracy, NLI-model accuracy (no CI run scores with a model), or the business correctness of any answer.

Repeated CI evidence on the private corpus is measured, not asserted (#560, VRC-14). `scripts/repeated_ci_evidence.py` reads the scheduled-run history of the RBOK lawbook E2E workflow from the GitHub Actions API and publishes a dated index of every run it counted, under `docs/regulated/evidence-index/repeated-ci-evidence/`. A run counts as one unit of evidence only if it completed green AND still holds an unexpired archived pack: a green run whose pack has aged out leaves nothing to re-inspect, so the chain decays on its own unless runs keep coming. A missed weekly occurrence breaks the chain, because runs that happened cannot vouch for weeks that produced nothing. Measured on 2026-09-04: 4 consecutive green runs against a target of 8, seven scheduled runs recorded and all seven green, five missed weekly occurrences between 2026-06-29 and 2026-08-10, and only two distinct corpus revisions across the whole recorded window — so what is proven so far is that the pipeline kept running green over a largely unchanging corpus snapshot, not that it holds across evolving inputs. **The claim is therefore locked**: the evidence ledger carries `EV-CI-REPEAT-001` as `requires_evidence` and `GAP-REPEATED-CI-EVIDENCE` as open, and the gate fails if any document in the tree states the claim while the measurement says otherwise, if the workflow loses its schedule or lowers its artifact retention, or if the published numbers stop matching a replay of the recorded runs.

A second domain vertical is shipped and gated (#565, VRC-22): `docs/regulated/domain-packs/eu-ai-act/` registers Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 as an authority anchor (CELEX `32024R1689`, ELI, OJ reference, each dated to the day it was resolved) and provides vocabularies for the obligation-bearing roles, the compliance activities, and the four risk tiers — 14 terms across 3 axes, each anchored BFO→IOF, the three axes declared disjoint. `nomos pack validate` runs the pack in CI on the real chain (3 presets resolved, a 4-document golden corpus producing 32 nodes), the same unchanged gate that runs the built-environment pack. The generality this evidences is that a vertical is a set of declarations rather than a fork of the engine; the D6 coupling metric is published as **two** numbers, because one would flatter it: 0 core paths required by the pack as it stands, and 1 core change to get there — the `risk_tier` facet axis, justified in ADR-0002. The pack is **pilot-grade**: it claims no conformity with the AI Act, classifies no real system into a risk tier, assesses no obligation, and substitutes for no conformity assessment, notified body or legal advice. Its golden corpus is **synthetic and says so in every document**; the regulation's text is reusable under Commission Decision 2011/833/EU, so the reason is fidelity rather than licensing — a near-miss quotation of a normative text reads as authoritative and is wrong, so the pack records where the text is authoritative and never what it says.

Cross-references between atoms are traced by rules, never generated (#579, VRC-43): `nomos atomize crossref` parses seven legally distinct cues — subject to, without prejudice to, notwithstanding, by derogation from, in accordance with, within the meaning of, and see-also — and emits each edge with the verbatim text it was built from and the half-open offsets it occupies in the atom. `VerifyCrossRefGraph` re-slices the atom and refuses any edge whose bytes do not reconstruct, any resolved edge whose destination is not in the atom set, and any unsupported entry with no reason; the engine verifies its own output before emitting it. A cue with no parsable target is recorded as `unsupported` rather than dropped, because silence would let a reader believe the rule stands alone, and a locator matching no atom stays `unresolved_target` with the locator kept verbatim rather than attached to a plausible neighbour. The three outcomes — resolved, unresolved, unsupported — are published separately. This claims that the text says what the edge records; it claims nothing about whether a cross-reference is legally correct, currently in force, or exhaustive, and no model participates at any point.

Facet vocabularies are validated as SKOS/RDF plus NOMOS predicates with standard SHACL (#580, VRC-44): `scripts/facet_shacl_gate.py` emits each pack's graph and validates it against `specs/shacl/facet-ontology.shapes.ttl` in CI using pySHACL. The shapes are portable SHACL, but the recorded execution is pySHACL only; no Apache Jena/TopBraid equivalence is claimed until a cross-engine record exists, and the graph does not claim to emit OWL `disjointUnionOf`. The shapes require a scheme that declares which axes are disjoint (declaring none would make orthogonality unfalsifiable, and that case is itself refused), an axis anchored to exactly one BFO root and one IOF class as real IRIs and carrying at least one term, and a concept with exactly one preferred label, scheme, axis, BFO mapping, IOF mapping and a pack-namespaced id. Orthogonality is a SPARQL constraint because it relates three nodes: a term sitting on two axes declared disjoint fails, naming the term and both axes. Both shipped verticals conform (built-environment 2 axes/10 concepts, eu-ai-act 3 axes/14 concepts). This claims conformance of the emitted graph to the shapes; it claims nothing about whether the BFO and IOF anchors chosen for a term are ontologically apt. The validation slice #580 is closed; static authoring/distribution is tracked separately in #643, while VocBench/Skosmos remain optional and ISO 25964 remains outside the current claim.

Computable atoms run on a borrowed substrate, and NOMOS builds no rule engine (#578, VRC-42): `nomos rule exec` hands each ```formula block, verbatim and with its source trace, to an external process over a versioned JSON protocol, and records what comes back. `cli/internal/ruleexec` contains no evaluator, no arithmetic and no expression parser; with no substrate configured nothing is produced at all — never a default, never a zero — and a substrate that is absent, crashed, timed out or off-contract fails closed with no partial record. A result for an atom nobody asked about, a duplicate answer, a missing answer, a `computed` with no value, an `unsupported` with no reason or carrying a value: each is refused. Every value travels with the expression it came from and its canonical ref, file and line range. The process boundary is what the licence register's `process_api_boundary` policy means in practice: NOMOS spawns a process and imports, links and vendors nothing; no engine is named in the Go code, and the reference adapter imports no third-party module. What is shipped is the boundary and a protocol-conformance fixture that evaluates one deliberately trivial arithmetic shape and answers `unsupported` for everything else — **no real rule engine is integrated**, no compatibility with L4/OpenFisca/Catala is claimed, and nothing here opines whether a boundary satisfies the AGPL. Persisted-result integrity remains bounded by #642: until request digests and returned values are fully bound and tamper-tested, do not treat a stored record as independently authenticated merely because the live protocol run passed. The persisted record is bound by three digests (#642): `request_digest` over what was asked and `results_digest` over what was recorded are both recomputed by `VerifyRecord`, so a formula edited afterwards or a value, unit, reason or status changed after emission stops verifying; `response_digest` covers the substrate's raw output, which the record does not retain, so it is evidence for a reader who kept that output rather than a self-check. None of the three is a signature: they detect a record that no longer agrees with itself, not one someone rewrote consistently, and the record says so in its own claim boundary.

A source fetched from the web carries its provenance or is refused (#610): the optional `web_source` block on a manifest entry records canonical and fetched URL, HTTP status, ETag and Last-Modified, the hash of the raw bytes as fetched and of the normalised text, the fetch instant, the crawler version, the scope policy, the robots and licence decisions, and the parent URL and depth. The manifest, the feed and `nomos check` validate it fail-closed with stable error codes: a web source without an address, without a stable `algo:hex` hash, without an RFC 3339 fetch instant or without a crawler version does not pass. An `undecided` robots or licence status may be **recorded** but is never **admitted**, and a page that is disallowed or out of scope may be written down but never enters a feed. The five natures the issue names — canonical content, external reference, binary or media, unsupported, excluded by policy — are the existing FSQ-02 admission fields, not a second vocabulary. What this claims is that the artifact says what was captured, from where, when and by what: a point-in-time capture, never the site's ongoing truth. Robots and licence decisions are recorded as the crawler made them, not adjudicated by NOMOS, and the legality of crawling any real site stays outside this contract.

An external corpus snapshot is consumed only if it is immutable and re-hashes to its own root (#611): the operational store — PostgreSQL, a crawler database — stays external and mutable and NOMOS never reads it; what it reads is an envelope (`nomos.external-snapshot.v1`) plus one JSON record per line, and `nomos corpus snapshot verify`, `snapshot import` and the `external-snapshot` section of the strict gate **recompute** the Merkle root over the records rather than reading it back. One changed byte, one record added or dropped, fails with `SNAPSHOT_ROOT_MISMATCH`; `immutable: false` is refused as a view, not a snapshot; declared counts are checked against the records, never trusted. Import keeps every `source_id`, `version_id`, locator, content hash and capture time, hands a web record's #610 provenance through untouched, records an inadmissible page as excluded with its reason rather than dropping it, and writes **no** manifest at all from a refused snapshot. The Merkle machinery is the body ledger's, already proven, so a record's inclusion is provable the same way a segment's is. The committed fixture was sealed by the real CLI, not written by hand. What this claims is that the records are the ones exported under that root — not that the export was complete relative to the store, nor that any record's content is correct.

Public canonical references are counted by what actually completed the chain, not by what the register lists (#644): `scripts/process_public_bibles.py` is driven by the register's own classifier, records a **dated hash-only capture** of each public URL (nothing stored, nothing quoted), and counts a reference as processed only when a local capture outside the repository carries exactly the recorded sha256 and scan, manifest, feed, body ledger, attestation **and** the strict gate all exit 0 over it. Every artifact is retained content-addressed outside the repository — a feed and a body ledger carry a third party's derived text — and a committed receipt names each artifact's digest, the capture's digest, the version identity and every stage's exit code, so the run can be replayed and compared byte for byte; one changed byte turns the capture into a named `capture_hash_mismatch` and marks the old receipt stale. Measured on 2026-09-05: 23 references classified public, 20 with a dated hash-only capture, 3 blocked at fetch with the reason recorded (one of them a 404 that exposes a stale register URL), **0 processed end to end**, and two in-repo policy documents exercising all six stages under their own name, never counted as external. "23 public bibles processed" is therefore not a sentence this repository makes.

The web-source chain runs end to end **offline** on a synthetic fixture (#612): a three-page static site, a deterministic normaliser (HTML → normalised text → Markdown) whose committed export is proven byte-identical to a fresh regeneration, JSONL records carrying #610 provenance and an `export_path`, an #611 envelope sealed by the real CLI, then `snapshot verify`, `snapshot import`, scan, feed, body ledger, `corpus attest --external-snapshot` and the strict gate, with the fixture proven untouched. The attestation names the web source type and the snapshot coverage — snapshot id, root, counts, number of web records — **measured from the verified envelope**, and a forged root refuses the attestation with no file written. One changed byte in one page moves that page's raw hash, normalised hash and Markdown and the snapshot root, and the drift check names the stale files before any stage runs; page chrome does not reach the normalised hash. None of this says anything about a real site: robots and licence are `allowed` by construction in the fixture, and Recursio itself, absent from this repository, is one conforming producer of the export contract, not a verified one.

A release **candidate** bundle can be assembled and verified, and that is all it is (#639): `nomos release candidate` binds one commit to its required artifacts (present **and** readable as their type), to the gate set measured green on that same commit, to every open blocking gap of the evidence ledger (all acknowledged, none acknowledged that the ledger has closed), to risks read from their measured source, to waivers and deviations — and refuses, writing nothing, on any rule (`CANDIDATE_*`). `approval_status` is always `pending`: an approved status, an approval entry or `release_executed: true` are refused because an approval is a human act under the release SOP (#561), never a tool output. The VRC-14 repeated-CI measure is recorded as a risk with its measured value and is never a precondition. `nomos release verify` replays offline, and a changed evidence byte, an edited gates file, a forged hash, gate or approval in the manifest, or a tree file changed after assembly is refused; the CI rehearsal proves the refusal on a tampered copy before archiving the candidate as a workflow artifact with read-only permissions. No tag, release, notes or approval are produced anywhere; a verified candidate says nothing about the release decision, the quality level or the acceptability of any gap.

NOMOS verifies a **supplied** Sigstore bundle offline against **supplied** trust material (#637), and that is the whole of the capability. `nomos attest verify-sigstore` hands the bundle, the trust root, the required artifact digest and the required signer identity to an external verifier behind a versioned process boundary (`tools/sigstore-verifier`, ADR-0005; the engine keeps three direct dependencies and the probe forbidding the Sigstore libraries under `cli/` stays green), then checks the verifier's word rather than trusting it: the digest it reports must equal the digest NOMOS computed, the reported signer must match the required identity, a `verified: true` with a non-zero exit is refused, only offline mode is accepted, and the exact response bytes are bound by digest into the record. Two real bundles serve as fixtures, one with an inclusion promise and SCT, one with an inclusion proof and signed checkpoint; a wrong digest, a wrong identity, a wrong issuer, a flipped signature, promise, certificate or proof byte, an edited checkpoint, or trust material that does not cover the log are each refused with no record, and with no verifier available NOMOS exits non-zero without a verdict. NOMOS does not obtain identities, does not write to any transparency log and does not vouch for the trust material's freshness; `sigstore_keyless` remains absent in the registry.

The facet vocabularies are **authored, distributable and validated to the NOMOS profile**, and nothing more (#643): the closed axes have an explicit, versioned SKOS authoring source whose value sets must equal the CUE-owned vocabulary or the build refuses, the open axes are read from the domain packs exactly as the SHACL gate reads them, and a canonical serializer builds byte-deterministic Turtle and JSON-LD plus an index and a static IRI→file mapping under `specs/generated/skos/`. The committed distribution is checked in CI to be a fresh build and to round-trip: every file is parsed back and must be isomorphic to the validated graph, pack graphs are re-validated against the shapes, every concept keeps its label and a mapping, and index hashes must hold. A lost label, a changed IRI, a dropped node, a forged hash, a missing mapping, a stale distribution after a pack edit, or an authoring value unknown to CUE each turn the tool red. No live SKOS service is operated, Skosmos and VocBench are possible clients rather than dependencies, ISO 25964 conformance is not claimed, and the ontological correctness of the BFO/IOF anchors is not asserted.

Keyless issuance exists only against **injected, non-production** Fulcio/Rekor endpoints (#645): `nomos attest sign-sigstore` refuses any Sigstore public instance before a single request is made, with no flag to allow it, contacts only loopback, reserved test domains or explicitly declared hosts, takes a supplied fixture identity token it never obtains itself, issues through the same process boundary as verification, checks the issuer's word (exit code, non-production mark, digest, identity, bundle present) and then verifies the produced bundle independently against the supplied trust material — a localhost fixture pair whose CA is generated at start, whose Fulcio reads an unsigned fixture token, whose Rekor is a one-leaf virtual log, and which runs no CT log, so the absence of an SCT is an explicit recorded decision rather than a default. Production, staging and unlisted hosts, a wrong expected identity or issuer, and a tampered issued bundle are refused; stopped services leave no partial bundle; a missing binary yields no verdict. Nothing here is a production issuance, a public identity or a public log entry: `sigstore_keyless` stays absent in the registry and #638 remains the regulated decision.

Nomos and Praxis now share a **verified evidence exchange contract**, and that is all it establishes (#660): the CUE contract fixes what crosses the boundary in each direction — Nomos artifacts by kind, path and hash with the verification state Nomos itself recorded, Praxis scenario evidence citing Nomos claims and atoms, runtime findings and CAPA status — and fixes the authority: Nomos produces, Praxis consumes, never the reverse. Regulated reliance is accepted only when every referenced Nomos artifact is verified with a record and an activation verdict is bound; anything else is not-qualified external input, usable for schema drift checks, dry runs and fixture design. `nomos evidence praxis-verify` re-checks shape, referential integrity, the reliance rule and the referenced artifacts' bytes against the tree, refuses unknown fields so no internal Nomos field leaks, and a JSON Schema mirror is proven equivalent on the fixtures by three independent checkers. The contract activates nothing: Praxis reliance stays blocked until the activation gate passes on verified artifacts, and passing that gate is itself not an activation.

The atom-to-Praxis mapping is **demonstrated and verified on a fixture**, nothing more (#661): the atom set is the real output of the atomizer over a synthetic golden source and is proven to regenerate byte for byte, the mapping carries only the fields the boundary document exposes and is a closed structure that refuses internal Nomos fields at load, its authority is fixed to Nomos, and `nomos evidence praxis-mapping-verify` requires every mapped atom to exist in the named atom set with the same content hash, canonical reference, type, domain and source position, to be approved on both sides so the mapping can never certify what Nomos has not, and to bind at least one Praxis check. An inverted authority, a leaked internal field, an unapproved atom, a stale feed hash, a drifted field or an atom changed after the mapping are each refused. This shows the evidence flow; it is not runtime assurance, not a compliance statement, and it does not activate Praxis, which stays gated.

Whether Praxis regulated reliance could be activated is now **computed, never declared** (#662): `nomos evidence praxis-gate` resolves every requirement of the activation record against the actual repository — signed decision records for acceptance, reconstruction and the qualified-corpus strict gate, signed acceptance blocks on the installation and operational qualification records, the production journey pack's overall result, a validation inventory with no unverified and unwaived high or critical item, the evidence contract's document status, and signed human review records — and treats anything unknown, unsigned or missing as not met, by name. The verdict is `blocked` with its reasons or `activatable`, never `activated`: the activation, its approvals and its record belong to the regulated roadmap, a record whose own status claims more than the checks support is refused as forged, and an exchange may claim regulated reliance only when it binds a verdict that is valid, unchanged and `activatable`. On this repository today the gate is blocked with eleven unmet requirements, and CI asserts exactly that as a tripwire; on a synthetic complete proof it opens, and it closes again when one review is missing or unsigned. None of this qualifies Praxis, activates anything or supports a joint claim.

The portfolio status is **computed from committed machine sources**, and is a view, not evidence (#667): `nomos portfolio status` aggregates the capability registry and its generated matrix (with the registry-to-matrix agreement recalculated row by row), the roadmap lanes and queues, the evidence-ledger gaps, the CAPA and periodic review records, the latest repeated-CI index, the Praxis activation verdict recomputed on the spot, the competence files, the domain packs, the public-source snapshots and, when supplied, a release candidate that must be pending. Every section names its source by path and hash with a freshness verdict against a stated policy; a source that cannot be read becomes an explicit unavailable section with its reason, never a silent omission; the contract is a closed structure that refuses any narrative field. On this repository today the evidence ledger, dated 2026-05-02, is reported stale rather than hidden. Nothing in the view approves, validates or lifts a claim: a green view of an unvalidated tool is still an unvalidated tool.

The periodic QMS records are **indexed and guarded, not produced** (#669): a sidecar builds a committed index of the management review, internal audit, role assignment and CAPA records with their dates, hashes, counted decisions, actions, findings and assignments, the recorded action statuses, and every cited repository artifact with its existence; the guard refuses a record without identity, type or date, a decision without id, an action without id or owner, a CAPA without status, severity or opening or closed without a date, a cited artifact that no longer exists, or a duplicated record id, and CI refuses an index that is not a fresh build. Actions whose status is not recorded are counted, not hidden. An index of records is not a record: nothing here performs a review, closes a CAPA or approves anything, and the records remain the human acts the regulated roadmap owns.

## Claim Levels

NOMOS public claims are organised in increasing assurance order. Each level may only be advertised when the level below it is satisfied.

| Level | Meaning | Gating |
|---|---|---|
| `artifact-generated` | NOMOS produced the artifact (feed, attestation, fidelity proof report, TOC, lexicon, RAG metadata) without crashing and with the documented schema. | Existing `validate` and `canonical:check` gates. Active today. |
| `source-traced` | Generated nodes carry source spans and resolve to a recorded source manifest entry. | Source span emission and manifest hash check. Active today on `rbok-lawbook` profile feeds. |
| `source-integrity-proven` | The corpus integrity report for this build is present and passes (coverage, duplicate spans, junk content, feed linkage, RAG linkage). | Active for recorded POC runs that carry the passing evidence pack; not a platform-wide claim. |
| `full-fidelity-proven` | Source-integrity-proven AND the corpus integrity check is wired into the blocking strict release gate for this build. | Active only for recorded POC runs where the strict gate evidence explicitly supports it; not a platform-wide claim. |

A build may not advertise a level until the gating evidence for that level is recorded and passing. POC-scoped results may carry the corresponding level only with explicit corpus and configuration scoping.

## Reserved Phrases

The following phrases are **reserved** and may only be written by NOMOS or about NOMOS where the cited evidence exists:

- `full_fidelity_proven` — requires a present and passing corpus integrity report wired into the strict release gate (`#346`). Today this phrase is permitted only as the recorded result of the legacy RBOK POC strict fidelity gate, scoped to that POC; it must not be lifted to platform-wide marketing claims.
- `full fidelity proven` / `complete fidelity` — same gating as above; not permitted as a platform-wide claim.
- `validated` (in a regulated sense) — reserved for customer-specific validation against intended use; NOMOS itself is not validated as a platform.
- `certified` — reserved for evidence of an external certification body decision; NOMOS is not certified.
- `compliant` (as a platform) — reserved for an explicit compliance decision against a named framework with a recorded record of evidence; not used for NOMOS at this release.
- `regulated-ready` — permitted with the documented meaning ("regulated-readiness structure and evidence workflow"), never as a synonym for "regulated".

Every emitter of these phrases (release notes, README, attestation text, marketing copy, CLI output) must check this list and the corresponding claim level before publishing.
